Core Principles

What is Waste Management?

Waste management comprises the collection, transportation, processing, recycling, and disposal of discarded materials. Modern zero-waste strategies prioritize source prevention over downstream landfills.

📉

1. Reduce

Avoiding excessive packaging, opting for digital receipts, refusing free plastic promotional trinkets, and choosing bulk groceries with minimal wrapping.

🔄

2. Reuse

Re-employing items for their original purpose or novel tasks. Glass pasta jars become pantry canisters, and old shoe boxes become closet organizers.

♻️

3. Recycle

Transforming sorted clean scrap materials into brand new commodities. Rinsing cans and flattening cardboard are crucial to prevent batch contamination.

🍂

4. Compost

Aerobic biological breakdown of organic plant scraps, fruit rinds, and coffee grounds into nutrient-dense humus that enriches garden soil without synthetic fertilizers.

Segregation Guide: Understanding The Bins

🟢 Biodegradable (Green Bin)

Accepted: Fruit peels, vegetable scraps, tea leaves, coffee grounds, eggshells, garden clippings, non-glossy soiled paper napkins.
Why it matters: Diverts wet organics away from buried landfills, preventing methane gas formation and producing valuable soil compost.

🔵 Recyclables (Blue Bin)

Accepted: Clean plastic bottles (#1 PET, #2 HDPE), clean corrugated cardboard, aluminum soda cans, clean tin food cans, glass jars.
Crucial rule: Always rinse out residual liquids or grease before placing in the blue bin to prevent contaminating entire recycling loads.

🟡 Electronic Waste (Yellow Bin)

Accepted: Old mobile phones, broken chargers, USB cables, laptops, keyboards, damaged audio headphones, circuit boards.
Why it matters: Contains valuable precious metals (gold, silver, copper) alongside hazardous flame retardants and heavy metals.

🔴 Hazardous Waste (Red Bin)

Accepted: Alkaline & lithium batteries, paint cans, solvent tins, fluorescent light tubes, pesticide bottles, discarded expired medicines.
Crucial rule: Must be handed over to certified hazardous waste reclamation facilities to prevent toxic groundwater contamination.
